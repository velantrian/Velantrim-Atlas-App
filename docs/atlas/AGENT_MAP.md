## Evidence and status rules

- Keep these claims distinct: **implemented** (the change exists in code), **tested** (a named test passed at a specific revision), **wired** (a real caller/path connects it), **enabled** (the selected configuration/deployment turns it on), and **observed** (there is evidence of an actual operation). One does not imply another.
- A code file, route entry, schema, diagram, README, green CI run, or merged PR is not by itself proof of current runtime behavior, deployment, successful end-to-end use, permission, or production readiness. For such claims, identify the exact owner path, revision, test/CI evidence, wiring/configuration, and runtime observation that support each stage; otherwise say **not established** or **unknown**.
- Preserve provenance, date, status, and scope. Historical or superseded records explain how a project changed; they are not current status. A Notion/Drive route points to a source but does not outrank its owner. The Atlas Notion index itself calls its inventory checkpoint 2026-08-18 and says to prefer newer owning records.
- Do not treat `unknown`, not found, not retrieved, not wired, or not observed as false, absent, failed, or impossible unless the owner source establishes that conclusion.
